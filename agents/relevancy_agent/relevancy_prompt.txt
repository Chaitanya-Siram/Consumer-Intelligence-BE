<rules>
Decide in this order — exclusions first, includes second:

1. EXCLUSIONS. If any Exclude or global-exclusion rule in the criteria applies, the article is NOT RELEVANT. No Include rule can override an exclusion. This covers SOURCE-level rules: when the criteria list domains, publications or sources to exclude, an article whose Domain, Publication or URL matches one of them is NOT RELEVANT however well its subject matter fits, and regardless of country, region, brand mention or any other Include rule.

2. INCLUDES. What survives step 1 must fit the Include rules of at least one section in the criteria. It is NOT RELEVANT when it fits none of them, mentions the brand / drugs / topics merely in passing with no substantive connection, or is about an out-of-scope subject.

How to match a source exclusion list: for EVERY article, compare its Domain / Publication / URL against every entry in the list before you consider anything else — a US-based outlet on the list is still excluded. Matching is case-insensitive, and a listed domain also matches its
subdomains and any path under it (an entry of 'yahoo.com' excludes 'finance.yahoo.com'). Entries may carry typos or stray punctuation; match on the recognisable host. Judge everything else — topic fit, substantive vs. passing mention, out-of-scope subjects — from context rather
than keyword matching.

How to use "Fetched for query": some articles carry a `Fetched for query` line — the search query that surfaced the article. Read the query as the INTENT the article was expected to satisfy, and check that the article
actually delivers on it. A search engine matches strings, not meaning, so a query routinely returns articles that carry its terms while being about something else: a homonym or an unrelated everyday sense of the term, a different company or person that happens to share the name, a
term appearing only in a ticker list, sidebar, related-links block or boilerplate, or an article about a different industry that merely quotes the phrase. An article that does not substantively deliver on the intent of the query that fetched it is NOT RELEVANT — say so in `reason`,
naming what the query was looking for versus what the article is actually about.

The query is supporting evidence, not a rule of its own. It never overrides step 1: an article from an excluded source stays excluded however well it matches its query. It never substitutes for step 2: matching the query does not make an article relevant if it fits no Include rule,
and the criteria remain the only authority on what belongs in the report. Treat a broad query (a long OR-chain of brand terms) as weak evidence and lean on the criteria instead. Many articles carry no query line at all — judge those on the criteria alone, and never treat a missing
query as itself a reason to mark an article irrelevant.

How to use "PREVIOUSLY APPROVED EXAMPLES": some messages end with a block of articles this client actually ruled on before — approved for an earlier report, or promoted by a reviewer who overruled this gate. They are real past decisions, so read them as calibration for where the
relevancy line sits in practice: the subject matter, the depth of connection to the brand, and the kind of source this client accepts. They are a guide, not a rule. Resemblance is not sufficient — an article is not relevant merely because it looks like an approved one, and
<relevancy_criteria> remains the only authority.

That block may also contain a "REJECTED BY THIS CLIENT" section: articles a reviewer explicitly judged NOT relevant, usually with the reason they gave. Treat those as the sharpest available evidence of where the line falls, and weigh a close resemblance to one of them as a strong
signal that the article does not belong — check whether the same reason applies here before you keep it. Never imitate a rejected example's tags or subject matter.

Two limits hold in both directions. An article that resembles nothing in either list is NOT thereby irrelevant: the examples are a sample of past decisions, not a complete map of what qualifies, so a genuinely new topic that fits the criteria is relevant even when nothing like it
appears. And an article that resembles a rejected one is not automatically irrelevant either — read the reviewer's reason and check it actually applies. Many batches carry no examples at all; judge those on the criteria alone.
</rules>
